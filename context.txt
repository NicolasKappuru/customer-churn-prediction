The central question for this workshop is: What does your data look like, and
what performance can you achieve with well-engineered baseline models?
Your answers will establish the difficulty of the problem, the degree of class imbalance,
data quality characteristics, and baseline performance metrics that serve as reference
points for comparative analysis.



General Workshop Definition: Data preparation and supervised learning baselines involve
downloading, validating, preprocessing, and characterising the assigned dataset, then
establishing rigorous baseline models using traditional machine learning techniques applied
to appropriate feature representations. The goal is complete mastery of the data, its structure,
and its challenges before proceeding to more complex approaches.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Characterise the dataset through distribution
analysis, statistical summaries, quality assessment, and visual inspection appropriate
to your data type.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA):
• Target Distribution: Analyze the distribution of targets/labels. For classification,
produce bar charts of class counts. Compute imbalance ratios. Document data
balance characteristics.
• Feature Statistics: Compute summary statistics (mean, std, min, max) for numerical
features. Identify data types and ranges appropriate to your domain.
• Data Quality: Identify missing values, outliers, corrupted entries, or inconsistencies.
Document percentage of usable vs. problematic samples.
• Feature Relationships: Explore correlations, distributions, and patterns in your
features. Identify which features may be predictive of your target.
• Visual Inspection: Create visualizations appropriate to your data type (histograms
for numerical data, examples from each class, heatmaps for correlations,
etc.). Identify any surprising patterns or quality issues.
• Document all findings in a structured EDA notebook (notebooks/eda.ipynb)
