\section{Summary}

This work formulates the customer churn retention problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
The objective is to train a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent to select the best retention strategy
for a customer with a probability of churn. Although the available dataset does not contain labeled
data describing the outcome of different retention strategies, it provides sufficient customer and
service information to establish an environment where the agent can learn which actions can reduce
the probability of churn.



\section{MDP Formulation}
The MDP is composed of a state space containing customer features, an action set representing
different retention strategies, stochastic transitions based on the customer's acceptance of an
offer, and a reward function based on the reduction of the predicted churn probability and the
cost of the selected action. RL agent will learn using DQN or PPO to determine the action with 
the highest expected reward. 

\subsection{Problem Statement}

The objective of the MDP is to determine the best retention strategy for a customer according to
their characteristics and current probability of churn. The RL agent receives the customer's state
and selects an action from a set of possible retention strategies. The selected action can be
accepted or rejected by the customer, introducing stochasticity into the environment.

The customer's churn probability is obtained using a supervised learning model. This probability
is used by the RL environment to evaluate whether the selected retention strategy improves the
customer's situation. The objective of the agent is therefore to reduce the probability of churn
while considering the cost associated with some retention strategies.

The simulation that will represent this MDP, is made based on the KKBox case, for that reason, the states and
actions were built with these data. Also the supervised model selected for estimate the probability of churn, has 
some problems observed in the workshop 1, the actions proposal tried to be generic, but can has bias originated of the 
importance that supervised model gives to some features, is considered a limitation of this MDP.

\subsection{State Space}

The state consist in the customer features, the data processed generated in the preprocessing pipeline. 
Any row can be a state valid for an episode in the environment and added to the probability of churn. 
\\There are 3 sections: member data of the user like age, city, etc. Transaction data calculated with the historical transactions.
User logs data calculated with logs in the last 3 months and probability churn. 
    
For this reason, each state is represented as a vector of 60 elements:

\begin{center}
\texttt{State = [Members 8 + transactions 29 +  user logs 22 + churn probability 1 = 60]}
\end{center}


\subsection{Action Set}

The action set are five discrete retention strategies.
Each action is designed to target a different groups of customer profiles, the agent is encouraged to learn
context-dependent selection. Actions are validated against the customer's current subscription
status before being offered, and each is subject to a stochastic acceptance by the customer.

For this simulation is based in a optimiste scenario, because normally the customer not accept the strategies with the 
high probability these actions are proposal. 

\subsubsection{Send Auto-Renewal Reminder}

\textbf{Strategy proposal.}
The customer has disabled auto-renewal and their membership is about to expire (within 15 days).
The company sends a targeted communication — push notification or email — reminding the customer
to reactivate auto-renewal before the deadline. The strategy is only applicable when
\texttt{is\_auto\_renew = 0} and the expiry date is within 15 days of the feature cutoff.

\textbf{Simulation effect.}
When accepted, the environment directly sets \texttt{is\_auto\_renew = 1} on the customer's
most recent transaction record. No new transaction is appended. The features most directly
affected are \texttt{last\_is\_auto\_renew} and \texttt{autorenew\_not\_cancel}.

\textbf{Acceptance probability.}
The favorable case applies to recently active customers (\texttt{days\_since\_last\_log} $\leq 30$),
who are still engaged and therefore more receptive: \textbf{60\%} acceptance.
For inactive customers the probability drops to \textbf{10\%}, as they have likely already
decided not to renew.

\textbf{Cost.}
This action carries a cost of \textbf{1\%}, reflecting the minimal operational expense of
sending a digital communication.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Offer Loyalty Renewal with Discount}

\textbf{Strategy proposal.}
Long-standing subscribers (account tenure $> 730$ days, i.e.\ 2 years) with auto-renewal still
active are offered a loyalty reward: a 30-day renewal at the standard list price of 149 NTD
with a fixed 20 NTD discount, so the customer pays 129 NTD. The offer acknowledges the
customer's loyalty while creating a positive touchpoint before a potential churn event.

\textbf{Simulation effect.}
When accepted, a new transaction is appended with \texttt{payment\_plan\_days = 30},
\texttt{plan\_list\_price = 149}, \texttt{actual\_amount\_paid = 129}, and
\texttt{is\_auto\_renew = 1}. The features most directly affected are \texttt{last\_discount},
\texttt{average\_amount\_paid}, and \texttt{difference}.

\textbf{Acceptance probability.}
The favorable case applies to customers with 15 or more active listening days in the most
recent month, indicating high engagement: \textbf{90\%} acceptance. Customers with lower
activity are less motivated by a loyalty offer: \textbf{15\%} acceptance.

\textbf{Cost.}
This action carries a cost of \textbf{15\%}, representing the 20 NTD discount the company
absorbs on each loyalty renewal.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Send Content Re-Engagement}

\textbf{Strategy proposal.}
Customers whose listening activity has declined month-over-month receive a personalised
content recommendation campaign — curated playlists, new releases, or promoted content —
aimed at reactivating usage before the subscription lapses. The strategy is only applicable
when the song play count in January 2017 is lower than in December 2016.

\textbf{Simulation effect.}
When accepted, between 2 and 8 synthetic listening-session log entries are injected into the
customer's raw user logs, drawn randomly from a pool of 8 template sessions spread across
January 2017. Each entry's song counts are scaled to the customer's December 2016 daily
average to preserve individual listening patterns. The features most directly affected are
\texttt{trend\_songs}, \texttt{trend\_secs}, \texttt{last\_login}, and
\texttt{days\_since\_last\_log}.

\textbf{Acceptance probability.}
The favorable case applies to recently active customers (\texttt{days\_since\_last\_log}
$\leq 30$), who are still reachable: \textbf{80\%} acceptance. For customers who have been
inactive for longer, engagement is unlikely: \textbf{10\%} acceptance.

\textbf{Cost.}
This action carries a cost of \textbf{5\%}, representing the expense of executing a
personalised content campaign.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Offer Downgrade to 99 NTD Plan}

\textbf{Strategy proposal.}
Price-sensitive customers are offered a lower-cost 30-day subscription plan at 99 NTD,
reducing the financial barrier to renewal. The strategy targets customers currently on a more
expensive plan (\texttt{plan\_list\_price} $> 99$~NTD), prioritising retention over revenue
per subscriber.

\textbf{Simulation effect.}
When accepted, a new transaction is appended with \texttt{payment\_plan\_days = 30},
\texttt{plan\_list\_price = 99}, \texttt{actual\_amount\_paid = 99}, and
\texttt{last\_discount = 0}. The features most directly affected are \texttt{last\_plan\_days},
\texttt{average\_amount\_paid}, \texttt{last\_discount}, \texttt{sum\_plan\_days}, and
\texttt{average\_plan}.

\textbf{Acceptance probability.}
The favorable case applies to newer customers (account tenure $< 365$ days), who are less
committed to the current plan and more willing to switch tiers: \textbf{90\%} acceptance.
For longer-tenure customers, the downgrade is less appealing as they are already accustomed
to the existing plan: \textbf{15\%} acceptance.

\textbf{Cost.}
This action carries a cost of \textbf{15\%}, reflecting the permanent reduction in revenue
per subscriber that results from moving to a cheaper plan.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Do Nothing}

\textbf{Strategy proposal.}
No retention intervention is applied. The agent may select this action when no other strategy
is expected to yield a positive outcome, or when budget conservation takes priority.

\textbf{Simulation effect.}
The customer's state and raw data remain completely unchanged.

\textbf{Acceptance probability.}
This action is always executed with probability \textbf{100\%}; the stochastic acceptance
step is skipped entirely.

\textbf{Cost.}
This action has zero cost.

\subsection{Transition Dynamics}

The selected action directly affects the customer's state. However, before modifying the state,
the environment determines whether the customer accepts or rejects the offered strategy. This
makes the transition stochastic because the same action can produce different outcomes.

The acceptance probabilities are defined according to the type of retention strategy:

\begin{itemize}
\item Send Auto-Renewal Reminder: 60\% (favorable) / 10\% (unfavorable).
\item Offer Loyalty Renewal with Discount: 90\% (favorable) / 15\% (unfavorable).
\item Send Content Re-Engagement: 80\% (favorable) / 10\% (unfavorable).
\item Offer Downgrade to 99 NTD Plan: 90\% (favorable) / 15\% (unfavorable).
\item Do Nothing: 100\% (always accepted, no state change).
\end{itemize}

The transition function can be represented as:

\begin{equation}
s_{t+1} = f(s_t, a_t)
\end{equation}

The acceptance or rejection of an action is stochastic. However, once an action is accepted,
the modification applied to the customer's state is deterministic. Therefore, the same state
and the same accepted action produce the same resulting state.

\subsection{Reward Function}

The reward is the reduction in predicted churn probability discounted by the action cost, 
the agent search maximize the reward, if the reduction difference of probabilities is high the reward will be increase.
But some strategies can generate cost to company and can penalize the use of this for retain de the customer.  

\begin{equation}
r_t = \bigl(p_{\text{before}} - p_{\text{after}}\bigr)\,(1 - c_{a_t})
\end{equation}

where $p_{\text{before}}$ and $p_{\text{after}}$ are the supervised model's churn probability
estimates before and after the action is applied, and $c_{a_t} \in \{0,\, 0.10,\, 0.15\}$ is
the cost of the selected action. When an action is rejected the state is unchanged and
$r_t = 0$.

\subsection{Episode }

An episode starts with a customer from the KKBox dataset. The customer's initial
state is provided to the supervised learning model, which predicts the probability of churn.
This information is then provided to the RL agent.

The agent select the retention strategy with the highest expected reward.
The selected action is then evaluated by the environment, which determines whether the customer
accepts or rejects the offer according to the corresponding acceptance probability.

If the customer accepts the offer, the action modifies the customer's state. The supervised model
then predicts the new probability of churn, which is compared with the probability before the action
to calculate the reward. The episode terminates after this reward is obtained.

If the customer rejects the offer, the customer's state remains unchanged and the reward is zero.
The episode then terminates.


\section{Environment}

The environment is implemented as a \texttt{gymnasium.Env} subclass in
\texttt{workshops/w3/environment/env.py} (\texttt{KKBoxRetentionEnv}).

\subsection{Environment Setup}

The action space is \texttt{Discrete(5)}, one index per retention strategy.
The observation space is a \texttt{Box} of shape $(60,)$ in \texttt{float32},
composed of the 59 preprocessed customer features followed by the current
churn probability estimate.

Four auxiliary classes support the environment:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{CustomerSelector} (\texttt{supervised\_model/customer\_selector.py}):
builds a pool of up to 1{,}500 churned users sampled from the February cohort.
It reads member data, transactions, and user logs from the raw Parquet files
and serialises the pool to disk so subsequent runs skip the expensive I/O.

\item \texttt{PreprocessorCustomerData} (\texttt{supervised\_model/preprocessor\_customer\_data.py}):
replicates the Workshop 1 pipeline on a single customer dict, computing all 59
features from raw member, transaction, and log records. It loads the clipping
parameters and imputation values from the \texttt{development.json} artefact
produced during training, ensuring identical feature engineering at inference time.

\item \texttt{SupervisedModel} (\texttt{supervised\_model/supervised\_model.py}):
wraps the M3 XGBoost model saved in \texttt{workshops/w1/artifacts/sherlock\_w1/}.
It exposes \texttt{predict\_probability()}, which returns the model's churn
probability for a preprocessed feature row.

\item \texttt{ActionsManager} (\texttt{actions\_manager.py}):
validates which actions are applicable to the current customer state,
evaluates the stochastic acceptance check, and applies the accepted action
to the raw customer dict. Action metadata (name, acceptance probabilities,
cost) is declared in \texttt{constants.py} as a tuple of \texttt{RetentionAction} objects.
\end{itemize}

On construction the environment pre-computes an observation cache: every
customer in the pool is run through the preprocessor and the supervised model
once and the resulting $(observation, p_{\text{churn}})$ pairs are stored.
This avoids re-featurizing on every \texttt{reset()} call.

\subsection{Episode Initialization --- \texttt{reset()}}

Each call to \texttt{reset()} selects one customer uniformly at random from the
pool using the environment's seeded NumPy RNG. The cached observation and initial
churn probability for that customer are retrieved directly without re-running the
preprocessor. The customer's raw dict is stored in \texttt{self.customer\_data}
and \texttt{self.probability\_churn\_before} is set for use in the subsequent
\texttt{step()} call. The method returns the 60-dimensional observation vector.

\subsection{Transition, Reward, and Termination --- \texttt{step(action)}}

\texttt{step(action)} executes the selected retention strategy and terminates
the episode immediately (single-step episodes, \texttt{terminated = True}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Action execution.} \texttt{ActionsManager.apply\_action()} is called
with the current customer dict and the selected action index. Internally it
checks validity, draws a Bernoulli sample against the acceptance probability,
and — if accepted — modifies the raw customer dict deterministically
(appending a transaction and, for the re-engagement action, log entries).
It returns the updated dict and the action cost (0.0 if rejected).

\item \textbf{Re-featurization.} The updated dict is passed to
\texttt{PreprocessorCustomerData.preprocess\_customer()}, which recomputes
all 59 features from scratch. The supervised model then estimates
$p_{\text{after}}$ from the new feature row.

\item \textbf{Reward.} Computed as
$r = (p_{\text{before}} - p_{\text{after}})\,(1 - c_{a})$, where $c_{a}$
is the action cost. A positive reward results from a reduction in churn
probability; cost penalises expensive strategies.

\item \textbf{Termination.} The episode ends unconditionally after the single
step. The \texttt{info} dict returns the before/after probabilities, the
action index, and the cost for logging and evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Random Policy Baseline}

Before training any agent, a random policy verifies that the environment behaves
correctly under unconstrained exploration. The policy selects uniformly at random
among the valid actions for each customer and runs for 100 single-step episodes.
The baseline is implemented in \texttt{workshops/w3/baselines/random\_policy.py}:

\begin{verbatim}
python workshops/w3/baselines/random_policy.py --episodes 100 --seed 42
\end{verbatim}

(Table~\ref{tab:random_baseline}). Both seeds confirm that all five retention

\section{Agent Algorithm}

Both DQN and PPO were implemented and compared. Both algorithms handle the discrete action space
of 5 retention strategies over a 60-dimensional observation. DQN was selected as the canonical
method for discrete spaces, while PPO was included to evaluate whether an actor-critic approach
with explicit action masking could exploit the probabilistic structure of acceptance more effectively.

\subsection{Deep Q-Network (DQN)}

DQN is a value-based algorithm that approximates the action-value function $Q(s, a)$
using a neural network. For each customer state the network outputs one Q-value per
action, representing the expected cumulative reward. The agent selects the action with
the highest Q-value, directly learning which retention strategy yields the greatest
expected return from the current customer state.

Implemented in \texttt{workshops/w3/agent/agent\_dqn.py}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{DQN active hyperparameters (\texttt{DQNConfig}).}
\begin{tabular}{lp{7.5cm}r}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Hidden size               & Units in each hidden layer of the Q-network & 128 \\
Batch size                & Transitions sampled from the replay buffer per training step & 64 \\
Replay buffer capacity    & Maximum transitions stored for experience replay & 50{,}000 \\
Gradient clip             & Maximum gradient norm to prevent training instability & 10.0 \\
$\varepsilon$ start / min / decay & Exploration schedule: initial value, floor, and per-step multiplier & 1.0 / 0.05 / 0.995 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The discount factor $\gamma$ and the target network update interval are excluded because
every episode terminates after one step (\texttt{terminated = True}), so the Bellman target
reduces to $y = r$ and the target network's Q-values are multiplied by zero and never used.

\begin{itemize}

\item \texttt{QNetwork} (\texttt{agent\_dqn.py}):
fully-connected network with two hidden layers of 128 units and ReLU activations,
mapping the 60-dimensional state to 5 Q-values, one per action.

\item \texttt{ReplayBuffer} (\texttt{agent\_dqn.py}):
fixed-capacity FIFO buffer (50{,}000 transitions) storing
(state, action, reward, next\_state, terminated, next\_valid\_actions) tuples,
sampled uniformly at random each training step.

\item \texttt{DQNAgent.select\_action} (\texttt{agent\_dqn.py}):
applies an action mask over the valid actions for the current customer.
During training, $\varepsilon$-greedy exploration is restricted to those valid actions.

\item \texttt{DQNAgent.train\_step} (\texttt{agent\_dqn.py}):
implements Double DQN — the online network selects the best next action and the target
network evaluates its Q-value. Loss is Smooth L1.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)}

PPO is a policy gradient algorithm that directly optimizes the policy $\pi(a \mid s)$
— the probability distribution over actions given the current state. Rather than
estimating Q-values explicitly, PPO updates the policy to increase the probability of
actions that yielded above-average returns, while clipping large updates to keep
training stable. An actor network outputs action probabilities and a critic network
estimates the state value to compute the advantage.

Implemented in \texttt{workshops/w3/agent/agent\_ppo.py}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{PPO active hyperparameters (\texttt{PPOConfig}).}
\begin{tabular}{lp{7.5cm}r}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Hidden size          & Units in each hidden layer of the actor-critic network & 128 \\
Clip $\varepsilon$   & Clipping threshold for the PPO surrogate objective ratio & 0.2 \\
Entropy coefficient  & Weight of the entropy bonus to encourage exploration & 0.01 \\
Value coefficient    & Weight of the value loss relative to the policy loss & 0.5 \\
Update epochs        & Gradient passes over each collected rollout batch & 4 \\
Minibatch size       & Transitions per gradient update within each epoch & 64 \\
Gradient clip        & Maximum gradient norm to prevent training instability & 0.5 \\
Rollout size         & On-policy episodes collected before each PPO update & 128 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The discount factor $\gamma$ and GAE $\lambda$ are excluded because in single-step episodes
the return equals $r$ directly and the advantage reduces to $A = r - V(s)$ regardless of
$\lambda$, so neither parameter has any effect on training.

\begin{itemize}

\item \texttt{ActorCriticNetwork} (\texttt{agent\_ppo.py}):
shared MLP trunk (60$\rightarrow$128$\rightarrow$128, Tanh) branching into a policy head
(128$\rightarrow$5 logits) and a value head (128$\rightarrow$1 scalar).

\item \texttt{PPOAgent.select\_action} (\texttt{agent\_ppo.py}):
invalid actions are masked by setting their logits to $-\infty$ before constructing the
Categorical distribution. In evaluation mode the action with the highest masked logit
is selected deterministically.

\item \texttt{PPOAgent.update} (\texttt{agent\_ppo.py}):
receives a completed rollout batch, computes one-step advantages ($A = r - V(s)$),
and runs 4 epochs of minibatch updates using the PPO clipped objective plus an entropy
bonus (coefficient 0.01).

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Protocol}

Both algorithms share the same training infrastructure. A pool of 1{,}500 churned KKBox
customers is split 80/20 into training (1{,}200) and validation (300) sets. Each configuration
trains for 3{,}000 episodes. Validation is run every 250 episodes over 100 held-out episodes
without exploration. The checkpoint with the highest mean validation return is saved as
the best model.
