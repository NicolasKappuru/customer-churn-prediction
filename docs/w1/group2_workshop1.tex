\section{Executive Summary}
Customer Churn is a problem about customer discontinue a company's services or products. Machine learning techniques can be applied with purpose of predict if a customer
has probability of be churn, this information can help the company to take decisions for not lost clients. This work search explore different machine learning techniques
to compare and get the best approach to solve this problem.

\subsection{Dataset Source and Acquisition}

The dataset is the  \textbf{WSDM -- KKBox Music Service Churn Prediction Challenge},
publicly available through the Kaggle competition platform at
\\ \url{https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kkbox-churn-prediction-challenge}.
\\ The dataset was released by KKBox Co., Ltd.\ under the competition's terms of use, which permit academic and non-commercial research purposes. No paid subscription is required; a free Kaggle account is sufficient to access all files.
To reproduce this experiment, the following two steps are required:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Accept the competition terms:} Create a free Kaggle account, navigate to the competition page, and accept the data usage rules. This step is mandatory before any file can be downloaded.

    \item \textbf{Download and place the raw files:} Download all nine \texttt{.csv.7z} archives provided by the competition and place them, \textbf{without extracting}, in the directory \texttt{workshops/data/}. The preprocessing pipeline extracts and converts them automatically during execution.
\end{enumerate}

No further manual setup is required. The data pipeline reads the compressed archives directly from \texttt{workshops/data/} and writes the intermediate \texttt{.parquet} files to \texttt{workshops/data/parquet/}. The final processed cohorts are produced in \texttt{workshops/w1/export-tabular-v5/}.

\subsection{Target Distribution}

In the February development cohort 63{,}471 of 992{,}931 customers churn (6.39\%), a majority-to-minority ratio of 14.64:1 (Figure~\ref{fig:w1_target}). In the March test cohort the rate is higher, 8.99\% (87{,}330 of 970{,}960 customers). The target is therefore strongly imbalanced, and the imbalance changes between months.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{figures/target_distribution_full.png}
\caption{Class distribution in the full February development cohort (\texttt{is\_churn}). Only 6.39\% of 992{,}931 users churn, yielding a 14.64:1 majority-to-minority ratio.}
\label{fig:w1_target}
\end{figure}

Two consequences shape the rest of the work. First, accuracy is uninformative: always predicting ``stays'' is 93.6\% accurate in February and 91.0\% accurate in March. The models are instead compared with log-loss, average precision (AP), and the precision, recall, F1 and Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of the churn class. Second, the relationship plots show the churn rate within each group against the overall rate (dashed line) rather than raw counts.

Many activity and payment aggregates are heavily right-skewed, so the median and the 99th percentile describe them better than the mean and maximum. The exploration of the raw tables (notebooks~02--05) adds the following observations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Members.} There are 21 cities and city~1 dominates. Age (\texttt{bd}) contains negative values and values above 100, and its median is 0 because a missing age was stored as 0. Gender is almost entirely empty, so it is not used as a feature. Registration has 18 channels, with codes 3, 4, 7 and 9 the most frequent.
    \item \textbf{Transactions.} Almost every plan lasts 30 days, although some plans reach about 450 days. The list price and the amount paid are almost identical; where they differ, the difference concentrates at 30, 149 and 180 and sometimes the customer pays more than the list price. Auto-renew is active in most transactions and cancellations are infrequent.
    \item \textbf{Listening logs.} The play counters have very long tails, and values above 1{,}200 songs in a day are implausible. Listening is bimodal: songs are either skipped before 25\% or played to the end. The number of records per user is also bimodal (very few or many).
    \item \textbf{Dates.} Registrations grow strongly from year to year, and transactions and expiry dates follow the 30-day renewal cycle. Time therefore carries information and is encoded as features such as tenure, recency and days to expiry.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Quality}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No member record.} 115{,}770 customers (11.7\%) have no member record, so \texttt{city}, \texttt{tenure\_days} and the registration fields are null for them. The flag \texttt{has\_member\_record} marks them.
    \item \textbf{No transactions.} 2{,}095 customers (0.2\%) have no transaction history and their transaction statistics are null. They are a very high-risk group: 31.6\% of them churn, almost five times the overall rate.
    \item \textbf{Invalid age.} The flag \texttt{bd\_missing} is 1 for 603{,}791 customers (60.8\%), whose raw age is invalid or absent. It is informative in itself: these customers churn \emph{less} (4.8\% versus 8.8\%), so the flag is kept next to the imputed age.
    \item \textbf{Duplicates and invalid counters.} Exact duplicate transactions are removed and negative play counts are set to zero (see the preprocessing pipeline).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Manual Segment Approach}\label{sec:enfoque_manual}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{figures/target_distribution.png}
\caption{Class distribution in the manual segment of the February cohort (\texttt{is\_churn}). The churn rate is 31.78\% (35{,}224 of 110{,}846 users), a 2.15:1 ratio --- far more balanced than the full dataset (14.64:1).}
\label{fig:w1_target_manual}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Challenges with the full dataset.}
The initial approach trained the three baseline models on the full KKBox cohort (992{,}931 February users, 6.39\% churn). The results were technically sound---ROC AUC of 0.863 and AP of 0.593 for the best model---but proved difficult to operationalise. The root cause was the structure of the data: 86.9\% of February users had auto-renewal active, and among them the churn rate was only 2.14\% (18{,}447 of 862{,}763). These users were difficult to identify as churners because their behaviour was largely indistinguishable from the vast majority who stayed. Many auto-renew customers exhibited superficial signs of disengagement---reduced listening, payment-method changes---without ultimately churning, introducing noise that the models could not resolve. The majority-to-minority ratio of 14.64:1 required aggressive threshold calibration, and even so, the best F1 for the churn class was only 0.584 on the March test set.

\paragraph{Motivation for the manual segment.}
The key observation was that churn is not uniformly distributed across subscription types. Figure~\ref{fig:why_manual} shows the churn rate and churn concentration by subscription state for February and March 2017. The manual segment stands out in two ways: (i) it has by far the highest churn rate among the large states (31.78\% in February, 40.74\% in March), far above the auto-renew rate (2.14\% and 3.63\%); and (ii) it is the most stable signal across months, with the rate increasing coherently from February to March rather than fluctuating. The \texttt{cancel} state has a higher rate (82.2\%) but only 9{,}075 users, making it too sparse for robust modelling.

Most importantly, 11.2\% of February users are manual subscribers, yet they account for \textbf{55.5\% of all February churners} (35{,}224 of 63{,}471). This concentration means that a model focused solely on manual users can recover the majority of real churn at a much more favourable precision-recall operating point.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/why_manual_segment.png}
\caption{Churn rate by subscription state for February and March (left), share of total churners by state in February (centre), and volume plus churn rate per state (right). The manual segment concentrates 55.5\% of all churners with a stable and coherent signal across months.}
\label{fig:why_manual}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Decision.}
Based on this analysis, the approach was revised to train, tune and evaluate exclusively on the manual segment. This eliminates the dominant auto-renew noise, reduces the class imbalance from 14.64:1 to 2.15:1, and focuses the model on the population where intervention is most needed. The full-dataset results are retained as a baseline for comparison in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\section{Preprocessing Pipeline}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────

The preprocessing pipeline implemented in this work is partially based on the public analysis published by Asad Rizvi~\cite{rizvi2018kkbox}, which includes an exploratory data analysis and a feature engineering approach for the KKBox Churn Prediction Challenge. From his work we adopted the general structure for joining the relational tables and several feature engineering ideas at the transaction and listening level. However, his approach targeted a different modelling objective and proposed a larger feature set with a different temporal framing. We discarded a significant portion of his proposed features, simplified the listening aggregation scheme, and redesigned the cohort structure around a month-over-month temporal split that better reflects a real production scoring scenario.

The full pipeline is implemented in \texttt{workshops/w1/w1\_data\_preprocessing.ipynb}. The model training and evaluation on the manual segment is in \texttt{workshops/w1/notebooks/w1\_manual\_data/w1\_manual\_tabular\_models.ipynb}.

\subsection{Raw Extraction and Parquet Conversion}

The nine \texttt{.csv.7z} archives in \texttt{workshops/data/} are extracted and converted to Parquet format by the \texttt{convert\_raw\_to\_parquet()} function (\texttt{workshops/w1/src/data\_processing/utils.py}), called in Section~2 of the preprocessing notebook. Each archive is decompressed one at a time using the \texttt{7z} binary, and only the columns defined in the project schema are retained and cast to compact integer or float types. The resulting files are written with \texttt{zstd} compression to \texttt{workshops/data/parquet/}. The conversion is idempotent: existing Parquet files are skipped, so the step can be safely resumed after an interruption.

Parquet was chosen over CSV because it is a columnar binary format that stores type information natively. Reading a single column from a large Parquet file does not require parsing the entire file, which reduces both I/O and memory usage significantly compared to CSV for the multi-million-row tables in this dataset.

\subsection{Cohort Definition and User Index}

The dataset is structured around two labelled cohorts defined in Section~3 of the preprocessing notebook:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{February development cohort} (\texttt{train.csv}): 992{,}931 users with features cut at 2017-01-31 and churn labels for February 2017. After filtering to the manual segment: 110{,}846 users, 31.78\% churn.
    \item \textbf{March test cohort} (\texttt{train\_v2.csv}): 970{,}960 users with features cut at 2017-02-28 and churn labels for March 2017. After filtering to the manual segment: 113{,}048 users, 40.74\% churn.
\end{itemize}

A shared integer index (\texttt{uid}) is assigned over the union of both cohorts, replacing the raw \texttt{msno} string for all subsequent joins. The manual segment filter is applied after feature assembly, using the subscription state computed by \texttt{\_common.py}: a user is classified as \texttt{manual} if their last payment had \texttt{is\_auto\_renew = 0} and their last day of transactions was not exclusively cancellations.

\subsection{Member Features}

Static demographic features are extracted from \texttt{members\_v3.parquet} in Section~4 of the preprocessing notebook. Invalid ages (outside the range [1, 100]) and negative registration channel values are imputed with the mean age and mode channel computed exclusively on the February cohort. Three derived features are added: \texttt{tenure\_days} (days elapsed from the registration date to the feature cutoff), \texttt{registration\_year}, and \texttt{registration\_month}. A binary flag \texttt{bd\_missing} marks rows where the original age was invalid or absent. This domain contributes \textbf{7 features} per user.

\subsection{Transaction Features}

Transaction records from both \texttt{transactions.parquet} and \texttt{transactions\_v2.parquet} are concatenated and deduplicated in Sections~5--6 of the preprocessing notebook. Exact duplicates—rows identical across all eight transaction fields for the same user—are removed, keeping only the first occurrence. Per-record derived columns are then computed: plan duration, plan coverage ratio, discount (list price minus amount paid), and behavioural flags. These records are subsequently aggregated into \textbf{one row per user}, yielding approximately 29 features covering counts, sums, averages, last-record attributes, and timing features relative to the cutoff (e.g., \texttt{days\_to\_expire}, \texttt{days\_since\_last\_transaction}). All transaction history up to the cohort cutoff is used; no fixed time window is applied.

\subsection{Listening Log Features}

Daily listening logs from both \texttt{user\_logs.parquet} and \texttt{user\_logs\_v2.parquet} are processed in Sections~7--8 of the preprocessing notebook. Records are restricted to the three calendar months ending at the feature cutoff and filtered to target users. Negative play counts are set to zero and non-finite \texttt{total\_secs} values are replaced by zero. The resulting user-day table is grouped to monthly sums and active-day counts. A daily p99.9 clipping limit is applied to the per-day mean of each listening counter—these limits are fitted exclusively on the February cohort and reused for March to prevent leakage.

The last three active months for each user are then pivoted into a wide row (\texttt{last\_*}, \texttt{last\_second\_*}, \texttt{last\_third\_*}). Several redundant per-channel columns are dropped, and four month-over-month trend features are added: \texttt{trend\_songs}, \texttt{trend\_secs}, \texttt{trend\_ratio100}, and \texttt{trend\_ratio25}. Recency features (\texttt{days\_since\_last\_log}, \texttt{has\_listening\_logs}) are computed relative to the cutoff. This domain contributes approximately \textbf{23 features} per user.

\subsection{Final Assembly and Saved Outputs}

The three feature domains are assembled in Sections~9--10 of the preprocessing notebook by left-joining member, transaction, and listening features onto the user list. Users absent from a given domain receive zero for all count and sum columns; presence flags (\texttt{has\_member\_record}, \texttt{has\_transactions}, \texttt{has\_listening\_logs}) mark whether a record existed. \section{Data Partitioning}

The split protocol is \texttt{cross\_month\_feb\_to\_mar} applied within the manual segment: the model is developed on February manual users and evaluated on March manual users, which mimics deployment where a model trained on past customers must score the next month. The manual segment filter is applied before the split, so all parts contain only manual users (Table~\ref{tab:w1_split}).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{llrrrp{4.0cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Part} & \textbf{Source} & \textbf{Users} & \textbf{Churners} & \textbf{Churn rate} & \textbf{Use} \\
\midrule

tune  & Feb manual (12.5\%)  & 13{,}771  &  4{,}399 & 31.94\% & threshold and model selection \\
\midrule
dev   & Feb manual (100\%)   & 110{,}846 & 35{,}224 & 31.78\% & development refit (all models) \\
\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Partition of the manual-segment data. February cohort (cutoff 2017-01-31) is split into fit/stop/tune using a stable hash of \texttt{msno}; March manual (cutoff 2017-02-28) is the test set.}
\label{tab:w1_split}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic assignment.} Each customer is assigned to a part from the existing split stored in the cohort export, so the assignment does not depend on row order and is reproducible across machines. It is stratified by the label, so the three February parts keep the same churn rate ($\approx$31.78\%).
    \item \textbf{Roles.} \emph{fit} trains the parameters of each model. \emph{stop} is used only by XGBoost to stop adding trees when the validation AUCPR stops improving. \emph{tune} is where the decision threshold is chosen and the winning model is selected. \emph{dev} (fit $+$ stop $+$ tune, all February manual) is used for the development refit after selection.
    \item \textbf{Test set.} March manual is never used to fit, tune or choose a threshold. It is used once, for the final metrics.
    \item \textbf{No leakage from preprocessing.} Imputation medians, scaling statistics and category encodings are learned on the fit data only; the stop, tune and test cohorts are transformed with those fixed values.
    \item \textbf{Final model.} After thresholds and the winner are chosen on \emph{tune}, each model is refitted on all of February manual (\emph{dev}, 110{,}846 users) before scoring March.
\end{itemize}

The churn rate rises from 31.78\% in February to 40.74\% in March, a substantial shift. Thresholds selected on February tune are therefore somewhat conservative for March, and this shift is part of the reason for the precision-recall trade-off visible in the results.

\section{Models and Configuration}

\subsection{Baseline Models}

Three models with increasing capacity are compared on the same 59 features and the same manual split.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{M1 -- Logistic regression.} Numeric features are median-imputed and standardised; the nominal codes are one-hot encoded (unseen categories ignored). Balanced class weights are used to compensate for the 2.15:1 imbalance. It is an interpretable linear reference. Configuration: $C = 1.0$, 1{,}000 maximum iterations.
    \item \textbf{M2 -- Random Forest.} An ensemble of decision trees trained on bootstrap samples with random feature subsets \cite{breiman2001random}. It handles non-linear effects and interactions without scaling and is robust to the redundancy between features. Configuration: 300 trees, \texttt{max\_depth = 10}, \texttt{min\_samples\_leaf = 5}, balanced class weights.
    \item \textbf{M3 -- XGBoost.} Gradient-boosted trees \cite{chen2016xgboost} with early stopping on the \emph{stop} part (up to 3{,}000 rounds, patience 100 on AUCPR). The positive class is up-weighted with \texttt{scale\_pos\_weight = 2.15} (the natural ratio in the fit split). Configuration: \texttt{learning\_rate = 0.1}, \texttt{max\_depth = 6}, \texttt{min\_child\_weight = 3}, \texttt{colsample\_bytree = 0.8}, \texttt{subsample = 0.8}. Because XGBoost requires pre-transformed data to pass an \texttt{eval\_set} for early stopping, the preprocessor is cloned and fitted on the fit split separately; the pipeline is not used for M3.
\end{itemize}

The key observations are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Average precision improves by 12.7 points.} AP measures the quality of the full precision-recall curve and is the most relevant metric when the goal is to flag a manageable subset of users for intervention. The improvement from 0.593 to 0.720 means that, at any recall target, the precision of the manual-segment model is substantially higher.
    \item \textbf{F1 improves by 9.5 points.} In absolute terms, the manual model identifies more churners per alert issued. This is partly a consequence of the higher prevalence (40.74\% vs.\ 8.99\%): the same precision-recall operating point yields a higher F1 when positives are more frequent.
    \item \textbf{ROC AUC decreases by 8.5 points.} This is expected: the strongest predictors in the full dataset were the auto-renew flags (\texttt{last\_is\_auto\_renew}, $\rho=-0.378$; \texttt{is\_auto\_renew\_change\_flag}, $\rho=-0.348$), which are constant in the manual segment. Without these features, ranking is harder. The manual model must rely on payment history, plan characteristics and listening behaviour.
    \item \textbf{The manual segment is more actionable.} Flagging 110K users at 32-41\% churn risk and reaching 69\% of them (at 0.5 threshold) is a more tractable intervention than flagging users from a 992K pool at 6-9\% risk.
\end{itemize}

The shift from full dataset to manual segment changes the information content of several features substantially. Table~\ref{tab:feat_degrade} summarises the features whose signal is lost or reduced.

The loss of the two strongest full-dataset predictors directly explains the AUC drop. In the manual segment, the dominant remaining signals come from:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Plan characteristics:} \texttt{last\_plan\_days}, \texttt{average\_plan}, \texttt{max\_plan\_days} --- longer plans are associated with higher churn even within manual users.
    \item \textbf{Payment method:} \texttt{last\_payment\_method\_id} --- certain payment methods still separate churners and stayers.
    \item \textbf{Payment history:} \texttt{transaction\_count}, \texttt{more\_than\_30}, \texttt{average\_amount\_paid} --- shorter history and higher payment amounts correlate with churn.
    \item \textbf{Timing:} \texttt{days\_to\_expire}, \texttt{days\_since\_last\_transaction} --- users closer to or past their expiry date are at higher risk.
    \item \textbf{Listening behaviour:} The listening features gain relative importance since the subscription-state signal has collapsed. Users with low recent activity (\texttt{days\_since\_last\_log}, trend features) are more likely to churn.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implications for future feature engineering.}
A purpose-built feature set for the manual segment could: (i) remove \texttt{last\_is\_auto\_renew} and \texttt{has\_transactions} (zero variance); (ii) replace the auto-renew-history features with more discriminative signals specific to manual users, such as how often the user switched between auto-renew and manual over their history; (iii) invest in richer listening-behaviour features, since those gain relative weight in this segment. The effective feature set would shrink to approximately 55--57 features, and a targeted search could likely improve on the current 0.72 AP.

\subsection{Model Comparison (Manual Segment)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{M3 (XGBoost with early stopping) is the best on log-loss and AP.} It achieves the lowest log-loss (0.5632) and the highest average precision (0.7195). Early stopping selects the number of trees automatically, acting as a form of regularisation that is well-suited to the near-balanced manual dataset.
    \item \textbf{M2 (Random Forest) is best on F1 and MCC.} It has the highest F1 (0.6792) and MCC (0.4518), and the best ROC AUC (0.7802). The difference from M3 is small ($\Delta$AP = 0.003, $\Delta$F1 = 0.007).
    \item \textbf{M1 (Logistic Regression) is competitive.} An F1 of 0.648 and AP of 0.691 on a near-balanced problem indicate that the signal is partly linear. The gap over M2 and M3 is real but modest.
    \item \textbf{XGBoost changed from worst to best.} In the full dataset, M3's probabilities were uncalibrated due to the large \texttt{scale\_pos\_weight}, giving a log-loss of 0.489 and an inflated threshold of 0.88. In the manual segment, the natural class ratio is only 2.15:1, so the probabilities are well-calibrated at scale\_pos\_weight $\approx 2$, and M3 achieves a log-loss comparable with M1 and M2 (0.563 vs.\ 0.569).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusions of Workshop 1}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The full-dataset approach produced a high-AUC model (0.863) but was difficult to operationalise due to extreme class imbalance (14.64:1) and weak signal among the dominant auto-renew population.
    \item The manual subscription segment, while only 11.2\% of users, concentrates 55.5\% of all churners and provides a stable, coherent signal across months (31.78\% in February, 40.74\% in March).
    \item Restricting the population to manual users improves AP by 12.7 points (0.593 $\to$ 0.720) and F1 by 9.5 points (0.584 $\to$ 0.679), at the cost of 8.5 AUC points lost due to the constant \texttt{last\_is\_auto\_renew} feature.
    \item Two features become constants in the manual segment (\texttt{last\_is\_auto\_renew} and \texttt{has\_transactions}) and three others lose substantial signal (\texttt{is\_auto\_renew\_change\_flag}, \texttt{autorenew\_not\_cancel}, \texttt{not\_autorenew\_cancel}). A purpose-built feature set for this segment is a natural next step.
    \item XGBoost with early stopping matches or exceeds Random Forest on this near-balanced problem without hyperparameter search, confirming that the calibration problem observed in the full-dataset experiment was an artifact of extreme up-weighting rather than a fundamental model limitation.
    \item The partition (fit/stop/tune from February manual, March manual as test) keeps the test month untouched and measures robustness to the shift in churn rate from 31.78\% to 40.74\%; this shift accounts for part of the recall shortfall observed in the results.
\end{itemize}
